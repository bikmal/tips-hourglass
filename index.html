Model equations

1. Flow is conservedQin = Qliver + Qshunt
2. Liver and shunt see the same gradientPSG = Qliver · Rliver = Qshunt · Rpath + Qshunt · Rwaist + K · ½ρ (Uwaist − Ustent)²
3. Calibration from the patientRliver = PSGpre-TIPS / QinQshunt,0 = Qin − PSGcurrent / Rliver    Rpath = PSGcurrent / Qshunt,0
4. Friction added by the narrowed segment (Poiseuille)Rwaist = ∫ (8μ / π) · (1/r(x)⁴ − 1/R⁴) dx
5. Exit loss as the jet re-expands (Borda–Carnot)Uwaist = Qshunt / (π rw²)    Ustent = Qshunt / (π R²)K = min(1, 2.6 · sin θ)    tan θ = (R − rw) / Lflare

Equation 2 is solved numerically for Qshunt; the predicted PSG follows.

PSGPortosystemic gradient (portal − RA pressure), mmHg
PSGpre-TIPSGradient before the original TIPS; sets liver stiffness
PSGcurrentGradient before reduction (values ≤ 0 treated as 0.5)
QinTotal portal inflow, held constant (default 2.5 L/min)
Qliver, QshuntPortal flow through the liver and through the TIPS
Qshunt,0Shunt flow before reduction
RliverResistance of the cirrhotic liver
RpathBaseline resistance of the whole shunt pathway (stent, ends, hepatic vein)
RwaistExtra friction resistance from the narrowed segment
R, rw, r(x)Radius of the original stent, of the waist, and at position x along the stent
LflareLength over which the stent flares from waist back to full size
θFlare half-angle; steeper means more abrupt
KExit-loss coefficient (0 = all energy recovered, 1 = abrupt expansion)
Uwaist, UstentMean blood velocity at the waist and in the full-size stent
μ, ρBlood viscosity (3.5 mPa·s) and density (1060 kg/m³)

Assumptions: steady Newtonian flow in rigid tubes; collaterals ignored; constant portal inflow makes the predicted rise an upper estimate; turbulence at very small waists is not modeled.
